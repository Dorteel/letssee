# Percepts

A percept says what one observer could make out at a particular time and from a particular frame. Keep one claim and its alternatives in `percepts/<id>.md`. Its source links and evidence locators should let a future reader find the pixels or joint measurements that prompted it.

Consider a camera frame of a blue cup partly hidden by the edge of a tray. A useful note might say “a curved blue edge is visible near the tray rim.” A cup lip or a colored ridge on the tray could explain the arc. The hidden outline leaves its origin ambiguous. A bounding rectangle in that image gives a later reader enough context to challenge the reading. A position in the room would require additional evidence.

Keep later observations from another angle as new percepts. An episode can connect the two views through their source links. If a decoder error affected the original interpretation, a corrected note can supersede it and explain the change. Both the first impression and the grounds for correction remain traceable.
