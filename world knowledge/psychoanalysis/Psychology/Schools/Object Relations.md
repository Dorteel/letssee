## Who / When
A plural psychoanalytic tradition developed during the 1920s–1950s, notably through [[Psychology/People/Melanie Klein|Melanie Klein]], [[Psychology/People/Ronald Fairbairn|Ronald Fairbairn]], and [[Psychology/People/Donald Winnicott|Donald Winnicott]]; 1930 is an approximate ordering date. [R1–R4]

## What
[[Psychology/Concepts/Internal Objects|Internal Objects]] and early relationships organize emotional life. Contributors disagreed about the roles of drives, fantasy, and actual caregiving. [R1–R3]

## Why
It addressed how enduring relationships and early dependence enter personality, beyond treating another person chiefly as a means of drive satisfaction. [R2, R3]

## How
Play, transference, and clinical interpretation investigate internalized relationships. Different accounts connect anxiety and defenses with early experience; this interpretive methodology does not itself establish causal validity. [R1, R2, R4]

## What changed
Relationships became increasingly central explanatory structures. Some accounts retained drives; others proposed a more fundamental relational motivation. [R2, R3]

## Developments
It developed within [[Psychology/Schools/Psychoanalysis|Psychoanalysis]]. It later influenced [[Psychology/Schools/Relational Psychoanalysis|Relational Psychoanalysis]]; the genealogical classification of that plural synthesis remains queued for review. Contrasts with classical drive accounts concern explanatory priorities, not chronological replacement. [R3–R5]
