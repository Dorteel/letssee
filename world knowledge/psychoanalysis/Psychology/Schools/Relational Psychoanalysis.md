## Who
[[Psychology/People/Stephen A. Mitchell|Stephen A. Mitchell]] was a central founder; [[Psychology/People/Jay R. Greenberg|Jay R. Greenberg]] coauthored its formative synthesis. [R1]

## When
Emerged in the 1980s; 1983 marks the published synthesis, not a single founding event. [R1]

## What
Psychic organization develops through relationships, including internalized patterns that remain accessible in treatment. This is the tradition’s theoretical commitment. [R2]

## Why
It sought an alternative to locating psychic structure chiefly in innate drives and their developmental transformations. [R2]

## How
Clinical inquiry examines [[Psychology/Concepts/Transference|Transference]] and [[Psychology/Concepts/Countertransference|Countertransference]], considering change within the evolving patient–analyst relationship and its context. [R1] [R3]

## What Changed
It integrated internal and interpersonal explanations and emphasized the therapeutic relationship as a setting of psychological transformation. [R3]

## Developments
[[Psychology/Schools/Object Relations|Object Relations]] and [[Psychology/Schools/Interpersonal Psychoanalysis|Interpersonal Psychoanalysis]] were predecessors. Classical drive-centered [[Psychology/Schools/Psychoanalysis|Psychoanalysis]] supplied a rival explanatory model; this does not imply opposition to every psychoanalytic approach. A plural tradition rather than one doctrine, it has no separate successor asserted here. [R1] [R2] [R4]
