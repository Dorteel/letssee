## Who / When
[[Psychology/People/Alfred Adler|Alfred Adler]] established an independent movement following the 1911 separation from [[Psychology/Schools/Psychoanalysis|Psychoanalysis]]; institutional consolidation followed in 1912. [R1]

## What
The person forms an organized whole, pursuing significance within social relationships. [[Psychology/Concepts/Social Interest|Social Interest]] and [[Psychology/Concepts/Inferiority Feelings|Inferiority Feelings]] are central explanatory ideas. [R2, R3]

## Why
It challenged explanations centered on sexual drives, asking how perceived inadequacy, purposes, and social circumstances organize conduct. [R1, R2]

## How
Clinicians infer a [[Psychology/Concepts/Style of Life|Style of Life]] from early recollections, family circumstances, and recurring strategies; encouragement and collaborative dialogue seek more cooperative goals. These are theoretical and clinical commitments. [R2]

## What changed
Explanation emphasized goal-directed unity and social embeddedness rather than treating personality chiefly as conflict among drives. [R2]

## Developments
Its separation makes [[Psychology/Schools/Psychoanalysis|Psychoanalysis]] both an institutional predecessor and an intellectual rival. Educational and family applications continued within this tradition; no separate successor school is asserted here. [R1, R3]
