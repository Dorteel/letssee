## Who / When
[[Psychology/People/Carl Gustav Jung|Carl Gustav Jung]] developed this approach through the 1912–1914 separation from [[Psychology/Schools/Psychoanalysis|Psychoanalysis]]; 1913 is an approximate marker. [R1]

## What
The psyche includes personal and [[Psychology/Concepts/Collective Unconscious|Collective Unconscious]] processes. [[Psychology/Concepts/Archetypes|Archetypes]] organize recurring symbolic patterns. These are the school’s theoretical propositions. [R2]

## Why
It sought an account of symbolic, religious, and developmental experience broader than explanations organized around sexual conflict. [R1, R2]

## How
Dream interpretation, symbolic comparison, and [[Psychology/Concepts/Active Imagination|Active Imagination]] investigate unconscious material. [[Psychology/Concepts/Individuation|Individuation]] names the differentiation and integration of personality. [R2]

## What changed
It broadened the unconscious beyond personal biography and treated symbolic development as potentially constructive, not solely a disguise for repressed wishes. [R1, R2]

## Developments
[[Psychology/Schools/Psychoanalysis|Psychoanalysis]] is a predecessor and rival. Later British analysts integrated [[Psychology/Schools/Object Relations|Object Relations]] ideas: this is cross-tradition influence, not proof that either school descended from the other. [R3]
