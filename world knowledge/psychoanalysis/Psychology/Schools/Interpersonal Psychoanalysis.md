## Who
[[Psychology/People/Harry Stack Sullivan|Harry Stack Sullivan]], [[Psychology/People/Clara Thompson|Clara Thompson]], [[Psychology/People/Erich Fromm|Erich Fromm]], and [[Psychology/People/Frieda Fromm-Reichmann|Frieda Fromm-Reichmann]] were central founders. [R2]

## When
Developed during the 1930s–1940s; 1943 marks institutional organization at the White Institute, not the invention of all its ideas. [R1] [R2]

## What
Personality and distress are understood through interpersonal situations and cultural conditions. [[Psychology/Concepts/Anxiety|Anxiety]], security, and satisfaction organize its clinical explanations. [R2] [R3]

## Why
It challenged detached clinical practice and the institutional rigidity attributed to orthodox analysis, seeking greater attention to social experience. [R1]

## How
Detailed clinical inquiry and [[Psychology/Concepts/Participant Observation|Participant Observation]] treat the analyst as involved in the situation being investigated. These are interpretive commitments, not proof of therapeutic efficacy. [R3]

## What Changed
It emphasized the analyst’s human presence and reciprocal influence, incorporating social and cultural perspectives into psychoanalytic inquiry. [R1]

## Developments
[[Psychology/Schools/Psychoanalysis|Psychoanalysis]] supplied a starting tradition and an orthodox rival model. It subsequently contributed to [[Psychology/Schools/Relational Psychoanalysis|Relational Psychoanalysis]], alongside [[Psychology/Schools/Object Relations|Object Relations]]; that synthesis does not make the traditions identical. [R1] [R2] [R4]
