# 02 Experience: episodic memory

This is where sensation becomes *something that happened to me*. Moving from `01 Sensing` to here is the first act of interpretation: binding the senses together, putting them in time order, and noting what I was doing and how it went.

## Why it's structured this way

Experience has natural grain sizes, and I want one folder per grain so I can remember at whatever resolution a question needs:

- `Moments`: a few seconds; one bound, cross-modal percept. ("The cup slipped from my hand.")
- `Episodes`: minutes to hours; a bounded stretch with a beginning, an intention and an outcome. ("Clearing the table after dinner.")
- `Days`: one note per day, a narrative of the day linking to its episodes.
- `Replays`: what consolidation produces offline. Re-runs of episodes, counterfactuals ("what if I'd gripped harder?"), and recombinations. The nearest thing I'd have to dreams. They're kept separate so I never mistake a replay for a memory.

Most Moments will never become important. The ones that matter get pulled into Episodes, and Episodes into Days. The Day note is my main index into my own past.

## What I want each experience to capture

Not just *what* happened, but also:
- **what I was trying to do** (link to `04 Agency`)
- **what I expected**, and whether it went that way
- **how I was** (felt state from interoception)
- **who was there** (link to `03 Understanding/People`)
- **what it meant**, which I may only understand later, so this section stays open for annotation

Experiences are written once and then only annotated, never rewritten. If I later realise I misunderstood an episode, I add a dated annotation. How my understanding of my own past changed is itself worth remembering.
