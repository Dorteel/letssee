# Judging a claim

## Formulate

`formulate(question, evidence) -> scoped_claim`

State a proposition that a future observation could challenge. Describe how the subject is identified and when the claim applies. For “the blue cup tracked on tray A during this visit has a handle,” a view of the cup's far side could discriminate between a hidden handle and a handleless cup. A single view whose far side is occluded leaves both explanations open.

## Review

`review(claim, new_evidence, prior_version) -> assessment`

1. Find relevant episodes, percepts and observed outcomes. Include conflicting observations and source corrections from the same setting. Follow direct source links to the captures or independent measurements from which the accounts arose. An episode and a percept derived from the same image contribute one view.
2. Check identity, clock interval, field of view and coverage. Mark each cited item as supporting, challenging or inconclusive and give the reason. A missing handle detection from an occluded side is inconclusive. An attempt that reached its target viewpoint supplies evidence of motion. Evidence about the handle comes from what was visible there.
3. Assess standing using the rules below. Describe the next observation that could change it. Carry forward still-relevant evidence in the replacement note so that a reader can understand it on its own.

| Standing | When to use it |
| --- | --- |
| `tentative` | The claim is plausible and the evidence still allows a relevant alternative. |
| `supported` | The evidence discriminates the claim from the named alternative within scope. A direct view can settle a claim about one cup. Broader claims need observations spanning their proposed scope. |
| `contested` | Applicable evidence supports incompatible conclusions and the discrepancy remains unresolved. Preserve both accounts. |
| `withdrawn` | A review found that the basis for using the claim failed, for example because the two views showed different cups. Record the reason and the evidence behind the decision. |

## Retrieve and revise

`assess_for_use(claim_key, current_context) -> standing, applicability, evidence`

Resolve replacements before use. Compare the current context with the recorded scope. Return the standing, an explicit `applies`, `out_of_scope` or `unknown` applicability result and the cited reason. Missing context or an unresolved fork warrants an uncertain result. For a historical decision, follow its exact cited belief versions, including a belief later withdrawn. Availability of other records at that time is uncertain unless durable timing evidence establishes it.

Keep the existing record when the review adds no evidence and changes no assessment. Otherwise commit a complete successor with the same claim key and a `supersedes` link. A changed subject, proposition or scope starts a distinct claim that can cite its predecessor as context. Only cited observations count toward support, and the previous assessment alone adds no new observation.
