# Episodes

An episode preserves enough of a situation for me to return to it. When I remember seeing a blue cup on a tray, I want to know where I was standing, which part of the cup I could see and what happened when I looked again. A list of objects loses the change in viewpoint that made the second look informative.

Keep one account in `episodes/<id>.md`. Group percepts that share an identifiable setting and an interval on the same clock. Include an attempt when its recorded context ties it to the scene. A heading, short timeline and source links should let a reader reconstruct the encounter without opening every capture. Preserve the original views through the linked percepts. A conclusion expected to hold on a later occasion belongs in [beliefs](../beliefs/.README.md).

## Boundaries

Give each account an opening and a closing reason. An observed departure, a sensor gap and the end of the available recording have different meanings. Describe which occurred. The event bounds cover the earliest and latest timed evidence in this account. The robot may have entered the scene earlier or remained there afterward.

Later evidence may change the order or identity of events. Append a replacement with a `supersedes` link and preserve the older account for readers who need the view available at the time of a decision. For a new visit, write a new episode even when the same tray appears.

## Returning to a scene

Search by setting, event interval or words from the encounter, then inspect the timeline and its gaps. A planner comparing viewpoints can follow the entry for the second look back to its percept. A belief reviewer can trace two descriptions back to a shared camera frame before treating them as corroboration.
